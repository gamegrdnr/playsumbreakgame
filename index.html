How to Play

Learn the rules in seconds

  1. 1

    Find a match

    Spot two neighbouring circles with the same number. Neighbours sit above, below, left or right.

  2. 2

    Merge them

    Drag one onto the other, or tap one and then the other. They become one circle holding their total.

  3. 3

    Gaps stay empty

    The space you dragged from stays empty and nothing slides in, so think before you merge.

  4. 4

    Finish with one

    Keep merging until a single circle is left on the board.

Questions & Answers

Everything you need to know about Sum Break

1What is Sum Break?

Sum Break is a number puzzle played on a grid of circles. Merge two neighbouring circles with the same number and they combine into their total. The catch is that the space you moved from stays empty, so every merge changes which circles can still reach each other. Clear the board down to one circle to win.

2How do you play Sum Break?

Drag a circle onto a neighbour above, below, left or right that shows the same number, or tap one circle and then the other. The two merge in the space you dropped onto. From level 30, wildcard circles (+1, +2, +3, ×2 and ×3) join in and merge with any number. Use Undo to take back a move and Restart to begin the level again.

3Is Sum Break free to play?

Yes. Every level is free, with nothing to download and no account needed to start playing. It runs instantly in any modern web browser.

4Which level should I start with?

Start at level 1. Levels 1 to 10 use a small 3×3 board to teach the basics, then the grid grows through 4×4, 5×5, 6×6, 7×7 and 8×8, up to 9×9 for levels 90 to 100. Every level is unlocked, so you can jump ahead whenever you feel ready.

5Can I play Sum Break on mobile?

Yes. Sum Break is designed for phones first: drag with your finger, or tap one circle and then its partner. It works just as well on a tablet or a computer, with a mouse or keyboard shortcuts.

6What type of puzzle is Sum Break?

It’s a number-merging logic puzzle. Unlike games where tiles slide or new ones appear at random, nothing moves unless you move it and every board is fixed. That makes it pure planning: every level can be solved, and the challenge is finding an order that doesn’t leave a circle stranded.

7How many puzzles are available?

There are 100 levels across seven grid sizes: 10 at 3×3, 15 at 4×4, 20 at 5×5, 20 at 6×6, 15 at 7×7, 9 at 8×8 and 11 at 9×9. Every one is checked by computer to make sure it can be solved, and levels get harder within each size.

8What are wildcards?

Wildcards are the circles marked +1, +2, +3, ×2 and ×3. They merge with any number and apply their operation to it, so a 7 merged with a +1 becomes 8. Two wildcards can’t merge with each other. Numbers normally follow 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on, and when one doesn’t, it starts next to the wildcard that brings it back.

9How do hints work?

You get 3 hints per level. A hint makes the circle to move glow and draws an arrow showing which way to drag it. If your board can no longer be finished, the hint tells you how many moves to undo instead and doesn’t use one up. Solving a level gives you a fresh 3 the next time you play it.

10Is my progress saved?

Yes, automatically, in your browser on this device. Solved levels, best times, hints used and unfinished boards are all kept. Clearing your browser’s data for this site, or playing on a different device or browser, starts you fresh.

11Any tips for solving?

Start with the smallest numbers. The smallest number on the board can only ever merge with an equal neighbour or a wildcard, because everything else only gets bigger. Keep an eye on circles in corners and along the edges, which have the fewest neighbours. And be wary of a pair that looks easy: on harder levels some matching pairs are traps that leave another circle with no partner.
